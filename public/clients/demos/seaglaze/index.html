Company

Measure twice, cut once

Buying new or replacing existing windows can be an expensive and time-consuming job on any type of vessel. Seaglaze is here to bring its experience and knowledge to the process, from templating through to delivery of the completed units.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    As a bespoke manufacturer Seaglaze has a range of frame sections to suit all requirements and budgets. In this first stage the team works out what you need. Tell them as much as possible: timescale, budget, the boat.

  2. 02

    Specification

    Even if it starts as a ball park price, the aim is no nasty surprises when the final invoice arrives. By the end of this stage you have a draft specification and a price.

  3. 03

    Sizing

    It is essential that the windows, doors and hatches are the best possible fit. Seaglaze calls this Patterning: your old windows or templates are digitised, and production does not start until the team is 100% certain what it makes will fit.

  4. 04

    Manufacture

    Once specification and sizes are confirmed and a deposit received, production starts. Seaglaze says its lead times are among the quickest in the industry because it does its own metal work, painting and even toughens its own glass.

  5. 05

    Delivery

    Products are quality checked and despatched in their own crate by TNT, normally next working day, to your address or your boat builder. You receive an email with the tracking code.

  6. 06

    Installation

    After nearly five decades with the UK's leading boat yards, Seaglaze does what it can so whoever fits the products has the advice and assistance they need. Installation guides are available.

How we work

Everything under one roof

Everything under one roof

Seaglaze does all its own metal work and painting, and toughens its own glass. The Rackheath building has grown over the years, adding a dedicated despatch area, paint shop and R&D workshop.

CNC router

The CNC router is at the core of the design and production process. It digitises templates sent in by customers to give an accurate drawing, and cuts the aluminium panels used in all doors and hatches.

3D printer

Prototyping parts of new designs before fabricating the actual product reduces costs and ensures the correct product is produced first time, which results in shorter lead times.

R&D department

An in-house R&D department develops new and bespoke products, from an improvement on an existing design to an entirely new extrusion, working closely with customer service on custom products.

A family business

Founded in 1970. Still family owned.

Seaglaze Marine Windows was founded in 1970 by two families before being passed across to the Clayton family. Richard Clayton took over in the 1990s before retiring and passing it on to his son, Alastair Clayton, in the 2000s. Richard is still involved and sits on the board of directors with his wife.

The business has stayed in the same place at the Rackheath Industrial Estate, close to the heart of the Broads National Park on the outskirts of Norwich, but has expanded and grown over the years.
