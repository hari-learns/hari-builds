Seaglaze Marine

Measure twice, cut once

Buying new or replacing existing windows can be an expensive and time-consuming job on any type of vessel. Seaglaze is here to bring its experience and knowledge to the process, from templating through to delivery of the completed units.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    As a bespoke manufacturer Seaglaze has a range of frame sections to suit all requirements and budgets. In this first stage the team works out what you need. Tell them as much as possible: timescale, budget, the boat.

  2. 02

    Specification

    Even if it starts as a ball park price, the aim is no nasty surprises when the final invoice arrives. By the end of this stage you have a draft specification and a price.

  3. 03

    Sizing

    It is essential that the windows, doors and hatches are the best possible fit. Seaglaze calls this Patterning: your old windows or templates are digitised, and production does not start until the team is 100% certain what it makes will fit.

  4. 04

    Manufacture

    Once specification and sizes are confirmed and a deposit received, production starts. Seaglaze says its lead times are among the quickest in the industry because it does its own metal work, painting and even toughens its own glass.

  5. 05

    Delivery

    Products are quality checked and despatched in their own crate by TNT, normally next working day, to your address or your boat builder. You receive an email with the tracking code.

  6. 06

    Installation

    After nearly five decades with the UK's leading boat yards, Seaglaze does what it can so whoever fits the products has the advice and assistance they need. Installation guides are available.

We recommend the windows are installed by an experienced boat yard, though many DIY enthusiasts have successfully fitted Seaglaze windows themselves.

FAQ

Straight answers

The questions customers ask most about lead times, templates and carriage, from Seaglaze's own FAQ.

Why do you send aperture templates?

Seaglaze works to very close tolerance, so the dimensions need to be as exact as possible. A template lets you measure against the aperture and helps production run smoothly.

Why is it taking longer than the weeks quoted?

Seaglaze has over 1000 windows and doors in production at any point in time and does not start a job until it has all the technical information and your confirmation. Private customers pay a deposit first, and the quoted weeks start from then. The longest lead time is normally the glass, which takes 3 to 5 weeks.

Why is it expensive to make a single window?

A bespoke window needs a significant amount of administration and technical work, and every product is made by hand. There is a significant time saving in larger, repeat orders that is not possible in individual orders.

Why does crate and carriage cost so much?

Seaglaze uses TNT domestic and international courier service: reliable, professional and tracked. If you prefer your own carrier, responsibility passes to you from pick-up. Vans also visit the South Coast and local areas often, so a delivery may be able to ride along.

Can you promise my order will be ready by a certain date?

Sales staff will very rarely give promises because Seaglaze depends on suppliers it cannot control. Only in the last 48 hours of production are the glass, finish and components all in hand. A rapid production service exists and must be agreed when the job is set up.

Why do I have to sign a confirmation?

A busy sales department means miscommunication can occur. Your signed confirmation is a written record of exactly what you require, and tells the production team the work has been verified by you.
