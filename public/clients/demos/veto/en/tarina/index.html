The VETO story

VETO was born in 1949 from one man's genuine interest in sauna, combustion technology and new innovations.

  • 1949Arvo Oksanen designs the wood stove prototype, the first afterburner in a wood stove.
  • 1952Muko Oy is founded. Wood stove production begins.
  • 1965The electric stove is finished. Continuous production begins in 1966 as Veto Elektro.
  • 2003The VETO wood stove gets the best overall grade in a Tekniikan Maailma comparison.

Wood stove: afterburner, air channels, glass door

In the evenings Arvo Oksanen advised housing companies on heating boilers. He had the idea of applying the boiler afterburner to a wood stove.

The VETO wood stove got the market's first afterburner and vertical air channels, and the market's first glass door was developed for it. It was designed from the start for continuous firing.

Electric stove: round, open, stainless

The electric stove was finished in 1965 and continuous production began in 1966 as Veto Elektro. At its core was a built-in humidifier, the only one of its kind, answering the dry löyly of electric saunas.

When it reached the market it was the only electric stove made of stainless steel. It was designed round and to stand on its own legs.

Muko Oy, a Helsinki family company

Muko Oy is a family company founded in 1952 by Arvo Oksanen. Subcontract-based manufacturing was new in Finland. Today the company is led by the family's third generation.

Medals and comparisons

The wood stove has won, among others, a Vermeil medal at the inventors' exhibition in Brussels. The electric stove got the best grade, five stars, in a Tekniikan Maailma test (TM 12/1982).
