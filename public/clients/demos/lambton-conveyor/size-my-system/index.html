How the numbers work

Bins: each Lambton bin size (30', 33', 36', 42' and 48' diameter, 4 to 12 rings) has a published capacity in bushels, cubic metres, cubic feet and metric tons. The tool picks, for each diameter, the fewest rings that hold your volume.

Bucket elevators: 39 models are listed with bushels, tons and metric tons per hour. The tool shows the smallest models that reach your rate, with the headroom they leave.

Chain conveyors, loops, augers and cannons: capacities come from each product’s own table. If your rate is above a product’s listed maximum it is not shown.

Drying: the Ultra Dry table gives bushels per hour for each bin size and fan at 140, 160 or 180°F plenum temperature and 20, 25 or 30% moisture.

Common questions

How do I choose a grain bin size?

Start with the bushels you need to hold. In Lambton’s table a 36' bin with 8 rings holds 27,804 bu and a 48' bin with 8 rings holds 50,951 bu; wider bins add capacity faster per ring, taller bins keep the footprint small. The tool compares every diameter for your volume.

How do I choose a bucket elevator capacity?

Match the elevator’s bushels per hour to the rate you need to receive or load out, then leave headroom. Lambton lists 39 models from 225 to 40,000 BPH; the tool shows the smallest ones that reach your rate.

What does the elevator capacity assume?

The catalogue capacities are examples based on centrifugal discharge speeds for whole grains, 56 lb shelled corn or grain sorghum at 15.5% moisture. Slow centrifugal and gravity discharge speed capacities are available on request.
