Where Metco has come from

From a Taita sheet metal shop in 1971 to a 5,000 sqm plant and a second office in Auckland.

  1. 1971

    Where it all began

    Rising oil prices in the early 70s forced one business to close and another to open. With experience in stainless steel fabrication and oil-fired heating products, Bill Hansen and Brian Francis saw an opportunity for a new sheet metal engineering business in the Hutt Valley, so Metco Engineering was born at 150 Eastern Hutt Road, Taita. After about eighteen months Bill sold his shareholding to Nigel Messenger. The majority of their work in the early years came from the car assembly plants, and they quickly established a reputation for quality and exceptional service. After their number one customer was sold to a Chinese buyer in 2001 and moved off shore, they decided it was time for some new blood and a change of direction.

  2. 2002

    Paul and Brent buy Metco

    Brent and Paul took over the company, put their mark on it, and expanded it to new heights. After extensive experience in other engineering companies, they used the best practices they had learned and invested in cutting-edge equipment to compete in the sheet metal market. Their strategy was to partner with customers to innovate or re-design products by finding cost-competitive solutions without compromising function or quality.

  3. 2005

    Worldwide contracts start to be won

    The strategy worked, and Metco began steadily gaining contracts, including opportunities to supply global customers. This included window stays for a large international company, having successfully competed against their Chinese factory on cost-effectiveness. Metco expanded its capability to a wide range of engineering processes and materials, growing into four specialist manufacturing sites.

  4. 2012

    Dexion Commercial

    In 2011 Dexion Commercial began to look for key outsource manufacturers that could manage custom made steel furniture and shelving for the New Zealand and Australian markets. Mark O’Donnell, General Manager of Dexion Commercial New Zealand: “In the end we found in Metco a partner with the right capabilities, technology, pricing and expertise.”

  5. 2013

    Metco wins Business Excellence Awards

    The Hutt Valley Chamber of Commerce, sponsored by Westpac Bank, hosted a ceremony showcasing “the best of the best in the Hutt Valley business community”. Metco won the Excellence Award for Manufacturing and Distribution, and walked away with the Supreme Award, the top business award that year.

  6. 2014

    Takeover of Perry’s Engineering contract work

    With Perry’s closing shop, Metco’s offer was accepted to buy up and use their well established assets to serve their clients. This involved sorting and rationalising their stock, tools and machinery and integrating them with what Metco already had.

  7. 2016

    Service award and new premises

    Metco rationalised its multi-site operations and opened a new premises in Gracefield, bringing the operation under one roof. Metco was nominated for the 2016 Minister of Defence Awards of Excellence to Industry, established by the New Zealand Defence Industry Advisory Council to reward industry’s contribution to Defence.

  8. Today

    Where Metco is today

    With the right foundations in place of technology, processes and know-how, the company is looking to grow its product diversity, production volumes and client base. Metco is expanding its international market with a full service complement, from concept and design to assembly and transport, with all stages performed in-house. A Metco Auckland office is now open at Eden Terrace.
