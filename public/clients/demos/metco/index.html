Quote-ready

Six things that get a quote moving

Metco does not publish a price list. The price comes from your part. These six answers are what the people pricing it need, and you do not have to have them all: their own process page says “You don’t need to have all the answers or be the expert here.”

  1. 01

    Material

    Steel, stainless, aluminium, brass, copper or plastic components, to your specification.

  2. 02

    Thickness

    The laser cuts mild steel to 25 mm, stainless to 20 mm, aluminium to 16 mm, copper and brass to 8 mm.

  3. 03

    Quantity

    From one-off commissions to contract runs of tens of thousands of units.

  4. 04

    Drawing or file

    2D or 3D CAD in, or a sketch: Metco’s designers work in SolidWorks, JetCAM and GibbsCAM.

  5. 05

    Finish

    Sanding, rumbling, buffing, or powder coating in Dulux colours on parts up to 24 m long.

  6. 06

    When and where

    Delivered to Auckland, Christchurch and across New Zealand, or anywhere in the world.

Source: Metco, Our Process, step 2 (Imagine) and the capability pages.

Cost

How much does laser cutting cost?

The honest answer is that it depends on the part, and nobody at Metco has published a rate to quote you. What the site does show is what moves the price. Four of those, in Metco’s and its customers’ words:

  • Setup time

    Metco’s automation cuts laser setup to 22 seconds per sheet, against 1.5 minutes without it, with lasers busy 85 to 90 percent of the time.

    laser-cutting page

  • Quantity

    Pertronic was told its small orders needed a lot of setup, which made the parts expensive. Metco redesigned the parts so they needed less.

    Pertronic case study

  • Design

    Dyno went from nine parts to five on one assembly, and from half an hour to two to five minutes to assemble it.

    Dyno case study

  • Finish

    Hand-finishing was “too laborious, inconsistent, and expensive” for Designtree, so Metco changed how the part was cut and machined.

    Designtree case study
