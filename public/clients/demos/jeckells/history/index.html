Jeckells the Sailmakers' history by Chris Jeckells, Managing Director

Sailmakers since 1832

This is a very brief history in which I hope to give you an insight of how this family firm came about and what we have achieved.

1832

A fisherman who made his own sails

It all started with my great, great, great, great grandfather who was a fisherman based in Great Yarmouth, Norfolk. He was not a very good fisherman so when his sails wore out he couldn't afford to buy a new suit. His only option was to make his own.

It was not until he was regularly the first fisherman back to port did he realise that he had made his sails with the panels running in the wrong direction, according to thinking at the time. His fellow fisherman realised the potential of his sails because in those days the first smack back to land would command the highest price for their freshest catch.

He was bright enough to realise that making sails in a draughty shed was actually a better and safer way of earning a living than fishing in the cold waters of the North Sea. So in 1832 Jeckells the Sailmakers was born.

1900s to 1940s

To the Broads, and a job to sew anything

Jeckells continued to service the fishing fleets of Lowestoft and Great Yarmouth until the early 1900's. By then smacks had started to decline but the pleasure sailing craft of the Norfolk Broads started to increase. A workshop was opened in Wroxham, at the heart of the Broads. Initially the sail loft was just to repair the sails on Saturdays, which was the hire boats changeover day.

All the new sails were made in Lowestoft, and as getting the sails to Wroxham was difficult and time consuming in the 1930's, it became clear that it would be better to open a full workshop in Wroxham so in the mid 1930's the main factory and head office moved to Wroxham.

With World War 2 on the horizon and Jeckells having a reputation to be able to sew anything, we got the job of making camouflage netting, gun covers and latrine screens for the services. After the War sailcloth technology moved on and so had transportation around the world. It was this combination that enabled us to grow internationally.

1970s to 1980s

Computers, 3-D design and a laser

We started using computers such as the Hewlett Packard desktop with small magnetic strip cards in the early to mid 1970's. Research and development allowed us to aid our sail designers with computers in 1982. My father was a very knowledgeable sailmaker with a penchant for maths and geometry and wrote our own programmes.

My father became the first UK sailmaker to successfully fit a window into a sail, which was a great help as he was a keen racer and it stopped him from hitting people. He also designed the original Lazybones Cruising Chute.

We introduced 3-D design software in the early 1980's followed quickly by a laser robotic plotter. We were one of the first lofts in the country, if not the first, to have a computer controlled laser cutter. The laser would cut with great accuracy and heat seal each cut to prevent fraying, and it also reduced the amount of space required to make a sail.

2004 to today

Our own sail loft, and a guarantee

We moved into our purpose built Sail loft in 2004 which, combined with our raised floor, new sewing machines and skilled staff, has enabled us to produce a perfect sail every time. Today we make very high quality performance cruising sails with a 5-year guarantee (again something that Jeckells was the first to introduce).

We have expanded into many other areas including deck up packages, full rigging service and of course our sail valeting and "within the week" sail repair service. We can collect a sail from anywhere within the UK on a Tuesday and guarantee to have the sail back by the weekend.

We also supply the Sailtainer in boom reefing system and the market leading headsail reefing systems. I hope that this gives you an idea of why I am so passionate about my company, staff and goods. Chris Jeckells

Four more stories from the history page

The wooden box of letters

My Grandfather had a wooden box in the corner in his study of approximately 3 by 3 by 4 feet high and every 3-4 months I was tasked with climbing into it. It would be full of letters and I was to check every envelope for mislaid cheques. I would then remove all of the foreign stamps so that we could give them to the local scouts or blind centre and it was truly amazing how many I would find.

The first job on the sailfloor

When I started on the sailfloor my first job was the most boring thing I have ever done. I had to knock eyelets into little ditty bags; each bag had 8 (sometimes 9) eyelets. After this my father made me work my way through every department so that I could learn about sails, their production and design.

A bucket with the bottom cut out

The prototype of our snuffer was a bucket with the bottom cut out and sewn onto a nylon tube. We modified the design to have a canvas funnel instead of a bucket and advertised it at the Earls Court boat show for £29.99 including VAT for a boat up to 28'0". We expected to sell a small handful if we were lucky, however we sold over 100 over the show.

Pioneering projects

I have been involved with pioneering various design projects and these have included the Tiki zip rig for the Wharram range of catamarans to supplying a snuffer at a realistic price.

Seven generations, and now a ninth

Chris Jeckells signs as Managing Director (7th Generation). In August 2023 Marine Business World reported that Mason, Chris's grandson, had become the ninth generation of the family to join Jeckells the Sailmakers, working in the sail loft on Saturdays and through the school summer holidays.

A message from Chris Jeckells

My family has been making sails since 1832. Today our craftsmen blend the traditional skills of sailmaking experience with our in-house CAD/CAM technology, and with our new purpose built sail loft we boast the most modern facilities available anywhere.

Our rugged, high performance cruising sails are made from the finest materials and are constructed to withstand the harshest conditions for many years. I am proud of our success and back that pride with our unbeatable five year guarantee.

We are equally proud of our attention to detail and levels of customer service. Wherever your boat is berthed we provide the service you would expect from a local company. We would be delighted to hear from you.

Yours Truly, Chris Jeckells
