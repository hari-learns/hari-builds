Every hand-off loses energy. We removed them.

A standard AC motor sends power through a gearbox, then belts and sprockets, before anything moves. Each step turns some of it into heat and adds a part that wears. A MagDrive is an outrunner: magnets on the outer shell turn around a fixed stator, so the drum itself is the drive. One transfer of energy, watts straight to torque. We call it Direct Drive Rotary Motion (DDRM).

Retire the servo stack.

Precise motion usually means a chain of parts, each one there to fix the last. A MagDrive does the same job in one sealed part.

A servo-driven belt today

  1. High-speed servo motor
  2. Encoder for feedback
  3. Planetary gearbox to trade speed for torque
  4. Servo drive to close the loop
  5. Mechanical hub with 14 or more bearings

A MagDrive

  1. One sealed direct drive
  2. No encoder, no gearbox
  3. A standard VFD in sensorless vector or PM mode
  4. No oil or grease above the food line

Five families, designed to fit your machine.

Every MagDrive runs on Direct Drive Rotary Motion (DDRM) and starts as one of five families, chosen by how it mounts. Then it's built to your machine: diameter, face width, shell, belt and connection.

1

Powered Hub

Mounted on one side: a cantilevered drive with the belt end left open.

Side belts, chicanes, alignment and diverts.

2

Powered Pulley

Mounted on both ends, for wider belts and higher torque.

Belt conveyors of every kind.

3

Powered Multi-Drive

Up to four drums on one shaft, each one controlled on its own.

High-speed merges, indexing, and repositioning product that arrives at random. An IDEA! Awards winner.

4

Powered Shaft

A hollow bore, an active shell, or a stationary shell with a turning shaft.

The hollow bore replaces a hollow-bore gearbox directly.

5

Powered Assembly

An array of drives on one superstructure, each driven independently.

Smart multi-belt feeders with as many as 54 hubs in one assembly.
