How a timing drive works

Timing belts are flat belts with a series of evenly spaced teeth on the inside circumference, combining the advantages of the flat belt with the positive grip of chain and gears.

The drive uses the positive engagement of two sets of meshing teeth, so there is no slip and no relative motion between the two elements, and the speed ratio stays constant.

That positive engagement makes these drives capable of transmitting large torque and withstanding large acceleration. The high torque drive (HTD) gives trouble-free, positive power transmission at lower speeds in high-torque applications.
