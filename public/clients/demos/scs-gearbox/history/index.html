The history of SCS Gearbox

Founded in 1979 in Bellevue, Ohio, SCS Gearbox began by manufacturing interchangeable gearing solutions for truck and tractor pullers, helping competitors quickly adapt their equipment to changing track conditions. Over the decades the company has grown alongside the racing industry, expanding its products to include quick-change gears, transfer cases, transmissions, axles and custom-machined components for applications from tractor pulling and circle track racing to monster trucks, off-road vehicles and marine racing.

Told from the company film on scsgearbox.com, with its own photographs.

  1. 1968

    Mike Sage graduated from high school. His inspiration came from his uncles Bill and Jim Tibboles during the 1950s and 60s.

  2. 1968

    Mike built the Ohio Express #1 at 18 years old on an English Ford Anglia with a 396 Chrysler Hemi.

  3. 1969

    Mike Sage joined the Army, becoming a Green Beret.

  4. 1971

    After returning from Vietnam, he started working at Midwest Tool & Die as a tool and die apprentice, where he received his experience in machining. As an apprentice Mike built hemi aluminum cylinder heads for the Front Engine Jr Fuel.

  5. 1972

    Ohio Express #2 was built to run as a UDRA match race car.

  6. 1977

    Mike Sage’s son Craig was born, and the second Arrow-body funny car, Ohio Express #3, was built from scratch to be a UDRA match race car. Mike was runner up to Frank Hawley and Frank Mendalline.

  7. 1979

    Mike Sage, Chuck Sage and Steve Callahan founded SCS Gearbox.

  8. 1980

    Mike and Chuck were approached by Dan Utz to build a multi-engine modified tractor. They developed the first quick-change gearbox for tractor pulling, known as the 20-Series.

  9. 1980

    SCS developed a complete line of quick-change transmissions for tractor pulling. A drop transfer case was developed for the 4x4 tractor puller to replace the stock transfer case.

  10. 1981

    The Sidewinder funny car was built at the beginning of the year. By the end of 1981, Don Garlits commissioned SCS Gearbox to build the top fuel dragster.

  11. 1983

    SCS Gearbox built the first bevel gearbox, used by Tim Engler.

  12. 1986

    Outcast II was built with Wheeler Ringer using two rear ends with one motor driving each rear end. A joystick controlled steering and power; the design helped the tractors go straight. In the same year SCS built the first successful CNC billet GMC roots supercharger impeller.

  13. 1987

    SCS built billet high-helix rotors and started building large drop transfer cases for monster trucks.

  14. 1987

    By request of Sherly Kears of Kears Speed Shop, SCS started producing 10 spline quick change gears for sprint and late model dirt cars.

  15. 1988

    SCS once again met a need with the design of a 7075 billet connecting rod.

  16. 1990

    SCS built the first all-billet supercharger with front discharge and the twin-engine alcohol dragster, and received the NHRA Winston Drag Racing 1990 Engineering Award.

  17. 1993

    Mike Sage received the 200 mph club plaque for 204.73 mph on 6/26/93 and was the Top Dragster Quick 8 Shoot Out winner.

  18. 1990s

    As the power increased in monster trucks, CV axles and better ball-and-claw axles, along with jackshafts and driveline components, were developed by SCS.

  19. Mid to late 1990s

    SCS moved into making the product line for marine applications, crashboxes and quick-change gearboxes.

  20. 1998

    SCS built the gearbox for the Twin Mills, which debuted at the 2001 SEMA Show.

  21. 1999

    SCS built the highly successful gearbox for the Dirtslinger tractor.

  22. 2007

    Mike’s son Craig and his wife Cindy purchased SCS Gearbox.

  23. 2016

    Craig and Cindy purchased Probell Racing Products to expand SCS Gearbox products into bellhousings.

  24. 2018

    Craig and Cindy purchased Innovation Engineering to expand SCS Gearbox products into drive plate assembly and repair, and marine flywheel.

  25. 2025

    Craig and Cindy purchased the Trick Titanium bellhousing line from Noonan Ultimate Race Engineering to add to the Probell Racing Products bellhousing line.
